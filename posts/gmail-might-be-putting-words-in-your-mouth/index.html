Gmail might be putting words in your mouth

When Gmail translates multi-language mails, it will rewrite sentences that already are in the target language, and potentially change their meaning

November 30, 2023
4 minutes
Michael Hertig
til

Earlier this week at the office, just a few minutes before I was about to head home, a coworker called me over (I'll refer to him as Charlie) to show me an email he received. He assumed this would be the kind of thing that I'd find interesting, or at least amusing. He was right about both.

The original email

Here is the original email Charlie received. I changed the names for privacy reasons, and added a few line breaks for readability purposes.

Salut Alice

Merci pour ta clarification. 

J'ai fermé le ticket. 

Mais ça reste la question de comment on va le 
faire dans le futur. 

@Charlie
Wie soll das in Zukunft gehandhabt werden? Alice 
konnte offenbar bisher bereits Zertifikate 
erstellen. (Und hat dies bisher auch so in 
den Projekten gemacht) 

Ich bitte um Klärung und Festlegung des Vorgehens.

Liebe Grüsse
Bob

As you can see, this email contains sentences of different languages. The email was sent from Bob to Alice, who speaks French. Charlie, who speaks German, was CC'd for a follow-up question.

Now, Charlie's Gmail account is configured to automatically translate French emails to German. Charlie is aware that Google Translate isn't perfect in translating whole sentences or large texts, but it is still better at it than he is, so he enabled the auto-translate feature for French a while ago.

The following text is what the translated (French → German) mail looked like in Charlies inbox.

Hallo Alice

Vielen Dank für Ihre Klarstellung.

Ich habe das Ticket geschlossen.

Aber es bleibt die Frage, wie wir das in Zukunft 
machen werden.

@Charlie
Was meinst du also mit Zukunft gehandhabt werden? 
Alice wird sich darüber ärgern, dass es 
zertifiziert ist. (Und hat dies bisher auch so 
in den Projekten gemacht)

Ich bitte um Klärung und Festlegung des Vorgehens.

Liebe Grüße
Bob

The translation error

To make it easier for everyone reading, I'll reference the text above in its English meaning from now on.

As can be seen in the full text above, Gmail decided to translate not only the French sentences to German, but also «translated» the German parts to German. And with that, it entirely changed the meaning of a sentence.

As you would expect, Charlie was slightly confused when he first read that sentence. And he was still confused after reading it a second, and a third time. Why would Alice be offended about a certificate? What is that about?

Because Charlie didn't understand what was going on, he called Bob (the sender) by phone to ask why Alice would be offended about a certificate. Bob, obviously, was dumbfounded by that question. After Charlie read him that sentence back, Bob insisted that he never wrote what Charlie was accusing him of writing. Shortly after, the underlying cause came to light. They both realized the email had been «translated».

Miscommunication

I'll admit, this is hardly the first case of miscommunication happening in workplace correspondence. And thankfully, Alice, Bob, and Charlie are all on good terms, so the miscommunication didn't lead to any further escalation or interpersonal conflict.

But depending on the individual relationships, and depending on the exact translation errors, it is easy to imagine that such an issue could lead to worse outcomes. And maybe it already has on some occasions.

Conclusion

While the instance presented here is rather funny, this miscommunication was caused for absolutely no reason. It occurred because Google translated German to German. Why would a user ever expect that to happen?

Personally, I would not consider this to be a highly concerning issue, but I think that it is important to know that this issue exists.

At a time when many users uncritically trust the technology they use daily, and at a time when big tech companies seemingly implement LLMs into every aspect of their products, errors like this really never fail to make me laugh.

The key takeaway here is: If you occasionally write or receive mails that contain text in multiple languages, make sure to disable the auto-translate feature in Gmail. And if you enable it, remember that you did, and check the original if the content is of high importance or confusing.
